# Narrative, Development, and Prototype

## Narrative Structure

The internal progression need not appear as formal chapters.

1. **The Fisherman:** ordinary work, engine use, port return, and starting-island routines.
2. **The Rumors:** conflicting stories and records complicate the parents’ disappearance.
3. **The Inheritance:** the fishing boat is revealed as the parents’ vessel with unexplained sailing equipment.
4. **The Threshold:** engines become inefficient; sails emerge as a faster, safer, fuel-efficient answer without being forced.
5. **The Sailor:** the player learns wind, trim, tacking, currents, stars, and weather.
6. **The Uncharted Sea:** mystical islands open in a largely free order.
7. **The Lost Civilization:** clues point to Atlantis and expand the goal beyond the parents.
8. **The Truth of the Loop:** the tsunami’s nature and its links to Atlantis, the parents, and sea become clear.
9. **The Final Choice:** the player decides what to do with a truth larger than rescue or treasure.

## Main Design Philosophy

Every system supports being a sailor uncovering a mystery. Ask of each mechanic:

> Does this deepen sailing, exploration, preparation, discovery, or the emotional relationship with the sea?

Crafting supports voyages; upgrades alter sailing style; skills improve interpretation and preparation; the loop creates temporal puzzles; islands teach navigation; narrative deepens curiosity; the boat connects the player to the parents; and the sea is the central character.

## Development Scope

The concept includes sailing physics, engine and wind navigation, waves, currents, weather, controlled procedural variation, mystical rules, touch gestures, gyroscope controls, crafting, upgrades, optional convenience skills, horizontal equipment, dialogue, island exploration, archaeology, a time loop, tsunami behavior, Android optimization, Godot, and C#.

The developer has already maintained a complex multiplatform business system spanning dashboard, point of sale, delivery notes, quotations, sales returns, receivables, work orders, goods collection, an image-prompt generator, purchasing and returns, orders, payables, products, stocktaking, inventory history, customer and vendor relations, financial reports, profit and loss, employees, audit logs, and settings. Its technologies include TypeScript web, Tauri desktop, Flutter mobile, Supabase cloud data, and SQLite local storage.

That experience indicates capability with modular architecture, complex state and business logic, APIs, local/cloud data, cross-platform work, maintenance, and expansion.

The new challenge is game-specific iteration: feel, physics tuning, responsive controls, animation, audio, level design, pacing, performance, experimentation, visual communication, and testing enjoyment rather than correctness. A business system can be correct when calculations are correct; physically correct sailing can still feel unpleasant.

## Recommended Prototype

Do not begin with twenty islands, Atlantis, the full story or tsunami loop, complex crafting, skill trees, trading, dozens of upgrades, or advanced archaeology.

Include only one boat, sea area, starting location, and destination island; an engine and a readable efficiency-loss zone where it remains usable; one sail; wind; basic waves; steering; deployment, trim, and tacking; one or two gestures; a stable elevated third-person boat camera; one small land area with four-angle 90-degree orthographic-style camera; optional gyroscope inspection; and simple audiovisual feedback.

The prototype answers:

> Is sailing enjoyable for ten minutes without story, rewards, or exploration?

> Can players read wind, current, momentum, and danger through third-person audiovisual and haptic feedback without numerical HUD elements?

> Is the stable camera comfortable for people sensitive to first-person motion?

If not, more islands, story, crafting, or upgrades will not repair the foundation.

## Final Identity of the Game

> A fisherman investigating the disappearance of their archaeologist parents gradually leaves the safe, engine-powered waters of human civilization and enters a supernatural sea where technology becomes inefficient and unreliable. By learning to sail through wind, stars, currents, sound, tides, and ancient knowledge, the player uncovers a chain of mystical islands, learns to survive or exploit their repelling tsunamis, and discovers evidence of a lost civilization known as Atlantis.

The central thematic contrast is:

> Technology can force a path through the known world, but knowledge, observation, tradition, and courage let the player travel farther, safer, and with the sea rather than against it.

The game begins as a personal search, becomes an obsession with the unknown, and ends with understanding why the parents were willing to disappear into the sea.

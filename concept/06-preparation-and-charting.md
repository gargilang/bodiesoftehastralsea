# Preparation and Charting

## Strategic Preparation

Voyage planning is separate from moment-to-moment sailing. At a navigation table or preparation screen, the player chooses destination, route, departure time, fuel, sail, tools, cargo distribution, food and water, speed versus durability, charts and notes, and preparations for storms, fog, night, or reefs.

This creates a strategic layer similar to preparing the Remnant in *Pacific Drive*, but information is imperfect. Forecasts should use natural language:

* Easterly winds are expected after sunset.
* Heavy fog has been reported north of the old lighthouse.
* The southern current is growing stronger.
* Fishermen report unusual silence near the western reef.
* Birds have begun migrating earlier than expected.

Avoid modern dashboard statistics such as exact storm probability, wind speed, or route safety percentages. The player should feel like a sailor studying signs.

## Charting

The map is not a GPS minimap. Navigation depends on memory, observation, and gathered information. Physical onboard charts can record dangerous currents, hidden reefs, safe passages, engine-efficiency thresholds, strange sounds, wrecks, underwater ruins, lighthouse bearings, fog entrances, low-tide routes, possible mystical islands, parental symbols, and tsunami exposure.

The chart is the equivalent of a ship log. Personal annotations may persist between loops, reinforcing that knowledge survives.

A minimap should be absent or very limited. Navigation instead uses island silhouettes, mountain shapes, lighthouses, clouds, sun, stars, compass bearings, waves, wildlife, sound, and manual notes.

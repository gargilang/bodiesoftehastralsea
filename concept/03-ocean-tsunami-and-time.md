# Ocean, Tsunami, and Time

## The Tsunami Expedition Loop

The recurring supernatural tsunami exists only within or around each mystical island. It is an island’s immune response to a foreign boat and explorer, not a global disaster or lethal reset.

When it arrives, it repels the player and boat toward home and begins the next expedition day. The player does not die; knowledge, charts, and narrative discoveries persist.

If caught away from the boat or capsized, the player washes up at home. Mystical-navigation components are damaged or lose efficiency and need repair, while the engine and ordinary-island systems remain usable to prevent a punitive soft lock.

An experienced player can recognize the signs, return to the boat, and ride the wave without capsizing. The tsunami then becomes a strategic route: the next expedition can begin near another mystical destination with little or no refueling. The event can therefore be setback, escape mechanism, or high-skill slingshot.

It must not be a supernova merely reskinned as a wave. Possible meanings include:

* the sea resetting itself
* the boundary around mystical waters closing
* Atlantis releasing energy
* an ancient disaster recurring
* the world replaying an unfinished event
* a mechanism protecting the lost island
* a consequence of something the parents activated

Each island needs a unique trigger and warning language rather than one generic timer. A lagoon may drain, a melody finish, lights extinguish, or a current reverse. Knowing the sequence lets veterans finish one more objective, retreat, or prepare to surf.

Warning signs can include birds leaving, whales changing course, fish surfacing, stopped wind, unnatural silence, receding water, exposed seabed, underwater lights, untouched bells ringing, clearer radio signals, active ruins, a darkening horizon, and smaller preliminary surges.

The final minutes remain interactive. Expeditions are framed as Day 1, Day 2, and so on—not Death 1 or Death 2. Homecoming supports maintenance and preparation; successful wave-riding preserves momentum.

## Time-Dependent World Changes

The cycle transforms islands and routes over time, creating temporal structure without moving islands. Examples include:

* caves available only at low tide
* ruins exposed shortly before the tsunami
* a wreck rising during a current shift
* flooded temples accessible early
* bridges exposed by receding water
* reefs becoming dangerous at particular times
* a lagoon opening after the first surge
* an island visible only in reflected tsunami light
* creatures migrating through the loop
* ancient mechanisms activating near the end
* currents reversing as the wave approaches

This creates urgency and route planning: knowing where something is does not eliminate the need to arrive at the right moment.

# Boat, Progression, and Economy

## The Boat as a Character

The persistent boat is companion, home, tool, and mystery, like the Remnant in *Pacific Drive*. It may have belonged to the parents before being repaired as a fishing vessel.

Apparently obsolete features can include an unused mast, sail fittings, strange storage, carvings, a nonstandard compass, old navigation table, sealed drawers, missing instruments, reinforced hull, unknown radio frequency, archaeology mounts, hidden chart room, false wall, and handwritten repair notes.

Exploration reveals its original purpose. Upgrading restores a family expedition vessel rather than merely increasing statistics, and damage matters because the same boat remains throughout the game.

## Upgrades and Crafting

Crafting, customization, and RPG-like builds are optional. A knowledgeable player can finish with the original boat and equipment. Prefer horizontal specialization to linear power: gear fits conditions rather than raising a universal score. Upgrades may be more forgiving, but old or mismatched equipment remains viable through skill.

Changing equipment at home should be quick; the strategy is what to bring, not inventory friction. Avoid numbered hull and sail ladders.

### Sail Examples

* **Large sail:** fast in light wind; hard to handle; dangerous in storms; stresses the mast.
* **Storm sail:** slow and stable; easy in strong wind; poor in calm.
* **Lateen sail:** effective closer to wind; technical; timing-sensitive.
* **Patchwork sail:** cheap and repairable; inconsistent; useful on resource-limited expeditions.

### Hull Examples

* **Heavy hull:** stable, durable, high-capacity, slow, and poor in shallows.
* **Light hull:** fast and responsive, but fragile and wave-sensitive.
* **Shallow-draft hull:** good for reefs and passages, less stable offshore, with limited cargo.
* **Reinforced expedition hull:** resists supernatural stress, costs more to repair, and sacrifices comforts.

### Other Modules

Rudder, mast, anchor, compass, lantern, radio, storage, water containers, repair bench, weather instruments, spyglass, mystical sonar-like tools, sail configuration, emergency and archaeology tools, fishing gear, and navigation table can support different builds.

Players might favor speed and storm aggression; durability, supplies, and safe range; or shallow water, archaeology, stealth, night travel, and acoustic detection.

Governing rules:

* no mandatory crafting gate
* no upgrade required for the true ending
* no strictly superior ladder
* no destination made impossible by loadout
* no installation friction that discourages experimentation
* knowledge remains more valuable than statistics

## Fishing, Rumors, and a Non-Grinding Economy

Fishing supports life, repair, preparation, and discovery without becoming a grind. Common ordinary-water fish provide modest money for fuel, supplies, and repairs. They spoil after one or two expedition days, discouraging stockpiling. Ordinary play should fund ordinary maintenance.

Rare fish are knowledge puzzles, not random loot. Rumors reveal habitat, timing, bait, behavior, or supernatural relationships; once understood, success should not rely on low-probability RNG. A specific NPC may exchange one for a specialized item, repair method, family story, chart clue, or optional upgrade. Afterward it is not a repeatable money or upgrade source.

Thus common fishing is a perishable livelihood, rare fishing is a one-time knowledge and relationship puzzle, and neither is required for the central mystery.

## Optional Skill Progression

A conventional tree may be unnecessary. If included, every skill is optional and improves interpretation, efficiency, accessibility, or convenience—not the world’s basic laws or completion access.

### Sailing

Clearer bad-trim feedback, faster ropes, easier recovery, reduced stamina use, and better heavy-weather balance.

### Weather Reading

Earlier storm warnings, clearer cloud cues, and more accurate pre-voyage forecasts.

### Navigation

Easier star identification, better chart annotation and landmark recognition, and clearer travel-time estimates.

### Carpentry

Faster, more material-efficient repair, stronger patches, and repair in rougher conditions.

### Archaeology

Better symbol translation, period recognition, false-artifact detection, and interpretation of parental notes.

### Fishing and Survival

Food preservation, fish-migration knowledge, efficient supply gathering, and reading wildlife for signs of land.

Skills modify playstyle but never replace understanding.
